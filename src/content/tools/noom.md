Noom is a habit-and-psychology approach to weight loss delivered through an app. Instead of leading with calorie math, it teaches the behavioral science behind eating — why cravings hit, how habits form, how to handle setbacks — through bite-sized daily lessons, then layers food logging and coaching on top. The goal is durable behavior change rather than a short-term diet.

The differentiator from trackers like MyFitnessPal or Lose It is the built-in curriculum and coaching, drawn from cognitive behavioral therapy. Noom's "traffic light" system labels foods green, yellow, or orange by calorie density so nothing is strictly off-limits, and the program adapts lessons, calorie budgets, and check-ins to what you log and the pace you set. It pairs the software with human coaches and peer groups, and has expanded into medication-supported weight management.

## Key Features

- CBT-based daily lessons on eating psychology and habits
- Traffic-light food categorization (green/yellow/orange)
- Personalized calorie budget with food, weight, and exercise logging
- Progress tracking with milestone check-ins
- Access to human coaches and support groups
- Program that adapts to your goals and logged behavior

## Pricing

- **Free trial**: Short trial (commonly around 7-14 days)
- **Subscription**: Monthly billing, with lower effective rates on multi-month and annual commitments
- **Add-ons**: Separate medication-supported program at a higher price point

## Best For

People who have tried conventional diets without lasting results and want to work on the mental and behavioral roots of eating habits, with structured lessons plus human and community support rather than a bare tracker.

## Limitations

Noom is a paid subscription and hard to justify over free logging apps if you only want to count calories. The daily lessons and logging take real time and consistency, and outcomes depend heavily on your own follow-through. It has also drawn complaints about its trial-to-paid conversion and cancellation process, so review the billing terms carefully before subscribing.
