Ideogram is a text-to-image generator whose defining strength is rendering legible words inside images — historically the biggest weakness of AI image tools, which tend to produce garbled, dreamlike lettering. Ideogram reliably spells out text, which makes it the practical choice for posters, greeting cards, book covers, memes, social graphics, and rough logo concepts where the words have to read correctly.

The company was founded by former Google Brain researchers who worked on image generation, and it competes with Midjourney, DALL-E, and Flux. While those rivals often win on pure photorealism or artistic detail, Ideogram differentiates on typography and design-oriented output. It adds a Magic Prompt feature that automatically rewrites a short prompt into a richer one, plus remixing, style controls, and aspect-ratio presets for different platforms.

## Key Features

- Accurate, readable text rendering within generated images
- Magic Prompt — automatic prompt expansion for more detail
- Photorealistic, illustration, 3D, and typographic styles
- Remix and variation tools to iterate on an image
- Aspect-ratio presets for social, print, and web formats
- Describe/image-to-prompt for reverse-engineering a look
- Free daily generations with faster paid queues

## Pricing

- **Free**: A batch of slow generations per day at no cost
- **Basic (around $7/month)**: A few hundred priority generations monthly
- **Plus (around $16/month)**: Around a thousand priority generations plus faster speeds
- **Pro/Team**: Higher limits and collaboration for heavier use

## Best For

Designers, marketers, and social creators who need AI images that include correct, readable text — posters, ads, thumbnails, mockups, and logo ideas — and anyone frustrated by garbled lettering from other generators.

## Limitations

For pure photorealism, cinematic lighting, or intricate artistic detail, Midjourney and Flux often produce more polished results. Ideogram's text is strong but not flawless — longer strings or unusual fonts can still misspell — so final logos and print pieces usually need a designer's cleanup. The free tier runs on slower queues with limited daily credits, nudging regular users toward a subscription.
