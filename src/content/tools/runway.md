Runway is one of the leading AI video platforms, used by filmmakers, music-video directors, advertisers, and creative studios. Its Gen-series models generate cinematic clips from a text prompt, a still image, or both, and the platform pairs that with a full editing suite — motion brush, inpainting, green-screen keying, and camera controls — so it functions as a creative tool rather than a one-shot generator. It competes with OpenAI's Sora, Google's Veo, Kling, and Pika.

Runway's reputation was cemented by real production credits — it has been used on major films and broadcast work — and by moving fast: newer Gen-4 models improved character and object consistency across shots, a longstanding weak point for AI video. The company also runs the AI Film Festival, signaling its focus on serious creators rather than casual novelty clips.

The trade-off is cost and length. Generations run on credits, clips are short, and building a coherent sequence means generating pieces and editing them together with a director's eye.

## Key Features

- Text-to-video and image-to-video generation
- Motion brush and camera controls for directing movement
- Inpainting and outpainting for video
- Green-screen keying and background removal
- Improved character and scene consistency in newer models
- Frame-level editing tools and API access

## Pricing

- **Free**: A one-time allotment of credits to try it out
- **Standard (around $15/month)**: A monthly credit pool for regular use
- **Pro (around $35/month)**: More credits and higher limits
- **Unlimited (around $95/month)**: Unlimited slower-queue generations for heavy users
- **Enterprise**: Custom pricing for studios and teams

## Best For

Filmmakers, creative directors, motion designers, and agencies who need professional-grade AI video and editing tools and are comfortable iterating to get a polished result.

## Limitations

Credits are the constraint — longer, high-resolution, or heavily iterated work adds up quickly. Individual clips are short, so full scenes require stitching, and quality depends heavily on strong prompts and reference images. For quick casual clips, cheaper tools like Pika may be enough.
