CapCut is a video editor developed by ByteDance, the company behind TikTok, and it has become the default editing app for short-form creators worldwide. It runs on mobile, desktop, and in the browser, with projects syncing across them. The appeal is that it packs genuinely useful AI features into a free, approachable interface rather than a professional NLE's steep learning curve.

Its AI tools target the exact needs of social video: auto-captions that transcribe your audio into styled, timed subtitles; AI background removal that cuts out a subject without a green screen; noise reduction for cleaner audio; and a deep library of templates where you drop in clips and get a trend-matched edit automatically. Newer additions include text-to-video, AI voice tools, and script-based generation. Because it shares an ecosystem with TikTok, exporting straight to social platforms is seamless.

One caveat worth naming: its ByteDance ownership has drawn regulatory scrutiny, and availability has fluctuated in some markets.

## Key Features

- Auto-captions with styling in many languages
- AI background removal without a green screen
- AI noise reduction and audio enhancement
- One-tap templates tuned for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts
- Text-to-video and script-to-video generation
- AI voice changer, text-to-speech, and vocal tools

## Pricing

- **Free**: The large majority of editing and AI features, with some export/asset limits
- **Pro (~$7.99/month, or discounted annually)**: 4K export, cloud storage, and premium templates, effects, and AI credits

## Best For

Short-form content creators, TikTokers, and Reels or Shorts producers who want fast, capable editing with AI captions and templates — without paying for or learning professional software.

## Limitations

CapCut is optimized for short social clips, not long-form documentaries or color-critical professional work, where Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve remain stronger. Some assets and higher-resolution exports sit behind Pro, and the free tier can add limits over time. The bigger consideration for some users and organizations is ByteDance ownership, which raises data-privacy and regional-availability questions that a Western-owned editor avoids.
