Synthesis began as an experimental enrichment program at Ad Astra, the private school Elon Musk set up for SpaceX employees' children, and grew into a consumer learning company. Its best-known product is Synthesis Tutor, an adaptive AI math tutor for young kids that talks with the child by voice, works through problems interactively, and continuously adjusts difficulty so the material stays challenging but not discouraging.

The original Synthesis offering was Synthesis Teams — live, collaborative problem-solving games where kids reason through open-ended challenges with peers, aimed at building judgment and strategic thinking rather than drilling facts. That focus on how children think, not just what they memorize, is the throughline across the brand.

Compared with mass-market tools like Khan Academy Kids, IXL, or Prodigy, Synthesis positions itself as more premium and reasoning-focused, leaning on its Silicon Valley pedigree and a distinctive game-based approach.

## Key Features

- Synthesis Tutor: a voice-based adaptive AI math tutor for young children
- Real-time difficulty adjustment tuned to each child's level
- Problem-solving games that reward strategy and reasoning
- Collaborative team challenges with other kids
- Progress updates for parents
- Curriculum rooted in the Ad Astra / SpaceX school experiment

## Pricing

- **Subscription (~$30–40/month)**: full access for one child, typically with an annual option
- **Family**: sibling or multi-child discounts available

Exact tiers shift over time; expect a monthly or annual per-child subscription with a free trial.

## Best For

Parents of elementary-age children who want to build genuine math intuition and problem-solving skills through an engaging, adaptive AI tutor rather than passive video lessons or flashcard drills.

## Limitations

Synthesis is a premium, fairly narrow product — it centers on math and reasoning for a younger age band, so it is neither a full K-12 curriculum nor a long-term fit as a child ages up. Budget-conscious families can get a lot of free math practice from Khan Academy; Synthesis's value rests on its adaptive tutoring and problem-solving philosophy being worth the subscription.
