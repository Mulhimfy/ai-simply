Interior AI is a room-redesign generator built by Pieter Levels, the solo maker behind Nomad List and Photo AI, and one of the earliest examples (late 2022) of shipping a profitable product on top of Stable Diffusion. You upload a photo of a real room, pick a style — Scandinavian, Japandi, Mid-Century Modern, Art Deco, Industrial, even Cyberpunk, among 40+ options — and it renders the same space refurnished and redecorated in that style, usually in under a minute.

The technical trick is that it doesn't just generate a random pretty room. Using depth- and structure-aware generation (ControlNet-style conditioning), it preserves your room's geometry — window positions, ceiling lines, layout — while swapping furniture, materials, and color palettes. That makes results feel like "my apartment, but renovated" rather than a stock render.

Interior AI also offers a virtual staging mode aimed at real estate: upload an empty room from a listing and get it furnished, at a fraction of the $30-100 per image that traditional virtual staging services charge. Competitors include RoomGPT (a simpler open-source take), REimagine Home, and full planning tools like Planner 5D — but Interior AI remains one of the fastest photo-to-redesign options.

## Key Features

- 40+ named design styles applied to your actual room photo
- Structure-preserving renders that keep windows, walls, and layout in place
- Virtual staging mode that furnishes empty rooms for property listings
- Multiple render modes and quality levels for rougher or more polished output
- Works on interiors of any type — living rooms, kitchens, offices, patios
- No software install; runs entirely in the browser

## Pricing

- **Free**: A handful of low-resolution, watermarked renders to try it out
- **Pro**: Paid subscription (roughly $20-40/month depending on billing cycle) with thousands of renders per month, high resolution, no watermark, and private uploads

## Best For

Homeowners testing renovation directions before hiring anyone, renters deciding on a furnishing style, and real estate agents staging empty listings cheaply. It's a mood-board machine that answers "what would my room look like as Japandi?" in sixty seconds.

## Limitations

Renders are visual inspiration, not buildable plans — the generated furniture doesn't exist in any catalog, dimensions aren't reliable, and busy rooms can come out with melted door frames or impossible built-ins. For shoppable recommendations or floor-plan-accurate work, an interior designer or a planning tool still wins.
