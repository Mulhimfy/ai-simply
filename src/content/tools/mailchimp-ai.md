Mailchimp is one of the longest-running and most widely used email marketing platforms, now owned by Intuit (which acquired it in 2021). Beyond email, it offers landing pages, forms, basic CRM, and marketing automation. Its AI features are branded under Intuit Assist and layered across the campaign builder rather than sold as a separate product.

The AI can draft subject lines and body copy from a short brief, generate on-brand content using your saved style, and recommend audience segments. Machine-learning features like send-time optimization and predictive segmentation aim to lift open rates by targeting the right people at the right moment. Mailchimp competes with Brevo, MailerLite, Klaviyo, and ActiveCampaign — and its main edge is brand recognition plus a very large template and integration ecosystem, not price.

## Key Features

- AI subject line and email copy generation (Intuit Assist)
- Send-time optimization powered by engagement data
- Predictive segmentation and audience insights
- Customer journey builder for automated multi-step campaigns
- A/B and multivariate testing
- Landing pages, signup forms, and basic CRM
- Large library of templates and third-party integrations

## Pricing

- **Free**: Limited contacts and monthly sends, Mailchimp branding
- **Essentials (~$13/month)**: A/B testing, more sends, remove branding
- **Standard (~$20/month)**: Automations, retargeting, AI content tools
- **Premium (~$350/month)**: Advanced segmentation and multivariate testing

Prices rise with your contact count, so real cost is often well above the entry tier.

## Best For

Small businesses, creators, and e-commerce brands that want an all-in-one, beginner-friendly marketing platform and value Mailchimp's ecosystem and integrations over rock-bottom pricing.

## Limitations

Mailchimp's contact-based pricing becomes costly as lists grow, and rivals like Brevo and MailerLite deliver comparable core features for less. For revenue-focused e-commerce automation, Klaviyo is often the stronger choice. Users also periodically cite deliverability and support frustrations as the account scales.
