Copyleaks is a content authenticity platform used mainly by schools, universities, publishers, and enterprises. It does two jobs in one pass: traditional plagiarism detection, comparing submitted text against a very large index of web pages and academic sources, and AI-content detection, flagging writing likely produced by ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and similar models. That combination is its main selling point over single-purpose tools.

Its competitors include Turnitin (the incumbent in academic integrity), GPTZero and Originality.ai (AI-detection specialists), and Grammarly's plagiarism checker. Copyleaks differentiates on multilingual coverage — it detects AI writing and plagiarism across many languages — and on being built for scale, with LMS plugins, SSO, and an API that lets institutions and platforms automate checks. It also offers source-level attribution, highlighting which specific sentences match which sources.

## Key Features

- AI-content detection across major models and many languages
- Plagiarism checking against a very large web and academic index
- Sentence-level source matching and similarity reports
- LMS integrations: Canvas, Moodle, Blackboard, and others
- API for automated, high-volume content verification
- AI-paraphrasing and source-code plagiarism detection
- Grading and gradebook tools for education

## Pricing

- **Free**: a small monthly page allowance for trial checks
- **AI Detector (~$10/month)**: AI-detection credits, billed by pages
- **Plagiarism + AI (~$11+/month)**: combined scanning, larger page volume
- **Enterprise/Education**: custom, per-seat or per-page institutional licensing

Pricing is credit- or page-based, so heavy use scales the cost up.

## Best For

Universities, professors, publishers, and content platforms that need one system for both plagiarism and AI detection, with institutional integrations and API automation.

## Limitations

As with every AI detector, Copyleaks can misclassify genuine human writing and can be fooled by heavy paraphrasing, so its scores should inform rather than decide academic-integrity cases. The page/credit pricing model also adds up quickly at volume, and its enterprise orientation makes it more tool than a casual writer or small blogger needs.
