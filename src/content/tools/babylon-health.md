Babylon Health was a UK-founded digital healthcare company that combined an AI symptom checker with video consultations from real clinicians. Founded in 2013 by Ali Parsa, it became one of the most visible names in health tech — powering the NHS-linked "GP at Hand" service in London and expanding into the US, Rwanda, and elsewhere. Its app let users describe symptoms to an AI triage chatbot, which suggested a likely urgency level and routed them toward self-care, a video appointment, or in-person care.

Importantly, Babylon's story is a cautionary one. After going public via a SPAC in 2021 at a multi-billion-dollar valuation, the company's finances deteriorated sharply. In 2023 its US operations filed for bankruptcy and its UK assets were sold off, effectively ending Babylon as a going concern. Anyone researching it today should treat it as a defunct or heavily wound-down service rather than an active product to sign up for.

## Key Features

- AI symptom checker and triage chatbot
- On-demand video consultations with GPs and clinicians
- Prescription and referral handling within the app
- Chronic-condition monitoring and a personal health record
- Historic integration with the NHS via GP at Hand
- Enterprise and insurer-facing health services

## Pricing

- **Historically freemium**: free symptom checking, with consultation fees or employer/insurer coverage for appointments
- **Status note**: after the 2023 collapse, pricing and availability are no longer reliable — verify current status directly before relying on any listed plan

## Best For

Primarily of interest now as a case study in AI-driven telehealth — its rise and fall — rather than a service to adopt. Historically it targeted patients wanting fast remote access to advice and people in areas with limited GP availability.

## Limitations

Beyond the company's financial collapse, Babylon faced sustained criticism from doctors and regulators over how safely its AI symptom checker handled serious conditions. The episode underlined a broader lesson: AI triage can widen access, but it is not a substitute for clinical judgment, and a compelling app does not guarantee a durable healthcare business.
