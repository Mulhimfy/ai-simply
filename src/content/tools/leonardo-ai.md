Leonardo AI is an image-generation platform aimed at creators who need control, not just a single pretty output. Where Midjourney gives you one polished house style, Leonardo exposes a large catalog of fine-tuned models — each trained for a look like anime, realism, RPG art, or product photography — plus editing tools that let you steer composition, pose, and style. It is especially popular with game developers and concept artists.

The standout tools are Image Guidance (upload a reference to control pose, depth, or edges, similar to ControlNet), the real-time canvas for painting and inpainting, and a consistent-character workflow so the same face or creature carries across many images. Leonardo also generates seamless textures and 3D-friendly assets, which is why studios use it for early concepting.

Leonardo was acquired by Canva in 2024, and its underlying Phoenix model now also powers image features inside Canva's own products. It remains available as a standalone app and API.

## Key Features

- Dozens of fine-tuned and community models for specific styles
- Image Guidance for pose, depth, and edge control
- Real-time canvas with inpainting and outpainting
- Consistent character generation across images
- Seamless texture and game-asset generation
- Motion tool for animating still images
- API access for developers and pipelines

## Pricing

- **Free**: A daily allowance of tokens that resets each day
- **Apprentice (around $10/month)**: Larger monthly token pool, faster queue, private generations
- **Artisan (around $24/month)**: More tokens plus upscales and higher concurrency
- **Maestro (around $48/month)**: Largest token pool for heavy users

Everything runs on tokens, so cost scales with how many high-resolution images and upscales you generate.

## Best For

Game developers, concept artists, indie studios, and designers who need consistent characters, specific art styles, and hands-on control over composition rather than a one-shot result.

## Limitations

The token economy is the catch — ambitious upscaling and batch work burn credits fast. And while control is excellent, the pure image fidelity of Midjourney's latest models is still a notch higher for photoreal work, so many artists use both.
