Sudowrite is an AI writing tool made specifically for fiction and creative writing rather than blog posts or marketing copy. It was built by novelists Amit Gupta and James Yu, and its features map to how fiction writers actually work: drafting scenes, describing settings, brainstorming plot, and revising prose. That focus is its main point of difference from general assistants like ChatGPT, Claude, and Jasper.

The centerpiece is the Story Bible, where you define your premise, characters, worldbuilding, and outline so the AI stays consistent as it drafts. Tools like Write (continue the scene in your voice), Describe (generate sensory detail), Rewrite (offer alternate phrasings), and Brainstorm assist at the sentence and scene level. Canvas provides a visual space for mapping story structure.

## Key Features

- Story Bible for characters, worldbuilding, outline, and style
- Write — continues your scene in your established voice
- Describe — sensory detail for a place, object, or moment
- Rewrite — multiple alternate versions of a passage
- Brainstorm — plot twists, names, and character ideas
- Canvas for visually mapping story structure
- Plugins and tuned prose settings for genre and tone

## Pricing

- **Hobby & Student (~$19/month)**: Entry credit allowance for casual writing
- **Professional (~$29/month)**: Larger monthly word/credit allowance
- **Max (~$129/month)**: The highest allowance for heavy, full-time use

Plans are credit-based, so heavy generation can exhaust a tier before month's end.

## Best For

Novelists, short-fiction writers, and screenwriters who want an AI collaborator that understands storytelling craft and keeps long projects consistent — not a general-purpose content generator.

## Limitations

For quick, one-off drafting, general models like ChatGPT or Claude are cheaper and nearly as capable, and Sudowrite's credit limits can bite during an intense drafting stretch. As with any AI prose, raw output tends to be serviceable rather than distinctive, so meaningful human editing is still required to produce work that reads as your own.
