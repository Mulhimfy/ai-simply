Asana is one of the most established work-management platforms, used across marketing, operations, and product teams to coordinate tasks, timelines, and cross-functional projects. Its AI capabilities — marketed as Asana Intelligence and, more recently, AI teammates — are layered on top of that existing structure rather than sold as a separate product. Because Asana already holds structured data about tasks, owners, and due dates, its AI can reason over real project state instead of free-floating text.

The headline features are Smart Summaries (turn a task's comments and updates into a concise status), Smart Fields and Smart Rules (auto-categorize work and automate handoffs), and Smart Status/Answers for portfolio-level reporting. It competes with Monday.com, ClickUp, Wrike, and — at the lighter end — Linear and Trello. Asana's edge is maturity and depth for larger organizations; its trade-off is complexity and price.

## Key Features

- Smart Summaries that turn task activity into status updates
- Smart Rules to automate handoffs, assignments, and notifications
- Smart Fields for automatic task categorization
- Smart Status and Answers for portfolio-level reporting
- Goals and OKR tracking with progress roll-ups
- Timeline, board, calendar, and workload views
- Integrations with Slack, Google Workspace, Jira, and Salesforce

## Pricing

- **Free (Personal)**: Core task management for small groups, limited members
- **Starter (~$13/user/month)**: Timeline, dashboards, and basic automation
- **Advanced (~$30/user/month)**: Portfolios, workload, goals, and more AI features
- **Enterprise / Enterprise+**: Advanced security, controls, and custom terms

## Best For

Mid-size to large teams running complex, cross-departmental projects who want AI to cut status-meeting overhead and surface what needs attention — and who already value Asana's reporting and portfolio depth.

## Limitations

The most useful AI features are gated to Advanced and Enterprise tiers, so the real cost of "Asana AI" is higher than the entry price suggests. For a small team or a solo user, Asana's breadth can feel heavy, and lighter tools like Trello, Todoist, or Linear are faster to adopt for simpler workflows.
