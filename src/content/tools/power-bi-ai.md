Power BI is Microsoft's business intelligence platform for building interactive dashboards and reports from business data. It sits alongside Tableau and Looker as one of the three dominant BI tools, and its biggest advantage is tight integration with the Microsoft ecosystem — Excel, Azure, SQL Server, and Microsoft 365. Authoring is done in the free Power BI Desktop app, with sharing and collaboration handled through paid cloud tiers.

Its AI capabilities center on Copilot, which can generate report pages, summarize data, and write DAX measures from plain-language prompts. Other AI features are built into the visuals themselves: Smart Narratives write text summaries of a chart, the Q&A visual answers typed questions, anomaly detection flags outliers in time series, and the decomposition tree helps drill into what's driving a metric. Copilot and the heavier AI features require Fabric or Premium capacity.

## Key Features

- Copilot for report generation, summaries, and DAX from prompts
- Q&A visual for typing natural-language questions against a model
- Smart Narratives that auto-write explanations of charts
- Anomaly detection on time-series data
- Decomposition tree for root-cause and drill-down analysis
- 200+ data connectors (Excel, SQL, Salesforce, Azure, and more)
- Row-level security and enterprise governance

## Pricing

- **Free**: Power BI Desktop authoring and personal use
- **Pro (~$14/user/month)**: Publish, share, and collaborate in the service
- **Premium Per User (~$24/user/month)**: Larger models and advanced AI features
- **Fabric / Premium Capacity**: Organization-wide capacity, full Copilot

## Best For

Business analysts and organizations already invested in Microsoft 365 and Azure who need governed, scalable dashboards and want Copilot to speed up report building and analysis.

## Limitations

The full AI experience — Copilot in particular — depends on Fabric or Premium capacity, which pushes real costs well above the Pro sticker price. DAX and data modeling carry a genuine learning curve, and teams outside the Microsoft ecosystem often find Tableau's visualization design or Looker's data governance a more natural fit.
